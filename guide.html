The European Accessibility Act, for small online shops

A plain-language summary. This is general information, not legal advice; check your national rules or ask a lawyer about your situation.

What it is

The European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882) requires many products and services sold in the EU to be accessible to people with disabilities. Most member states applied it from 28 June 2025, and 2026 is the first full year national authorities supervise it. Online shops (e-commerce services) are covered.

What "accessible" means for a website

The technical yardstick is the harmonised standard EN 301 549, which for websites points to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. In practice: text you can read (contrast), images with text alternatives, forms with labels, everything usable by keyboard, pages with titles and a language, and content that works with screen readers and zoom.

Who is exempt

Microenterprises providing services (broadly, fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet up to 2 million euro) are exempt from the service requirements, but not from the requirements for products they make or sell. Rules and definitions vary by country, so check yours before relying on this.

What can happen

Penalties are set by each member state and can be substantial, and complaints can come from authorities, consumer bodies and individuals. Some countries give a period to fix issues after notice. The first lawsuits under the Act were reported in France in late 2025.

A practical first week

  1. Scan your main pages (home, category, product, contact) to find what a machine can prove. Order a scan or use a free tool such as the axe DevTools browser extension.
  2. Fix the cheap, high-impact issues first: text contrast, image alt text, labels on form fields, buttons and links with names, page language and title.
  3. Test with the keyboard: can you reach and use everything with Tab, Enter and Space, and see where focus is?
  4. Try a screen reader on your checkout path (NVDA on Windows, VoiceOver on Mac).
  5. Publish an accessibility statement saying what you comply with, known gaps and how to contact you.
