We scanned 39 major EU online shops for accessibility failures. 87% had at least one.

An automated WCAG 2.1 AA scan of the home pages of 68 well-known online shops in Germany, France, the Netherlands, Spain, Portugal, Poland, Austria and Romania. 39 could be scanned; 85% of those had at least one serious or critical failure.

Method

One page load per site (the home page) on 2026-09-21 in headless Chromium at 1280px width, with axe-core 4.13 and the wcag2a, wcag2aa, wcag21a and wcag21aa rule sets, which is the level the European Accessibility Act points to through EN 301 549. Sites were requested once with an identifying user agent (details and opt-out), several seconds apart, and any site whose robots.txt disallowed automated access was skipped. Of 68 attempted, 0 were skipped for robots, 28 returned a bot wall and 1 timed out or errored. No site is named.

What this does not show

  • Only home pages were scanned, and only by an automated tool. Automated tools find just part of the WCAG requirements, so real failure rates are higher, not lower.
  • The sample is large, well-known shops that allowed automated access; it is not a sample of small businesses, and it excludes sites that blocked the scan.
  • Consent banners and overlays can hide parts of a page from the scan.
  • A failed rule is not by itself a finding of legal non-compliance.
